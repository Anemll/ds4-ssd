A compiler uses an abstract syntax tree (AST) as a structured, hierarchical representation of a source code's grammatical structure, stripped of syntactic details like parentheses or semicolons. After the parser performs lexical analysis and syntax analysis, the AST is built to capture the logical flow of the program—such as expressions, statements, and control structures—in a way that is easy to traverse and analyze. The compiler then uses the AST for semantic analysis, such as type checking and symbol resolution, and often transforms it into an intermediate representation (e.g., three-address code) for further optimization and code generation.
