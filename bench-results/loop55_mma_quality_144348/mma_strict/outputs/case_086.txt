Checksums catch accidental corruption because they rely on the mathematical property that even a single bit change in the data will almost certainly produce a different checksum value. When you compute a checksum (like a CRC or MD5 hash) over the original data and then later recompute it over the received or stored data, any mismatch between the two checksums immediately signals that the data has been altered.
